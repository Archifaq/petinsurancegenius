# Legal & Compliance Checklist (US, general information — not legal advice)

This file exists so Codex builds compliance in from the start, and so Claude has a
concrete checklist to verify against later. It is background research, **not** a
substitute for a licensed attorney reviewing the live site before launch — insurance
is a regulated vertical and enforcement has increased in recent years for lead-gen
sites specifically.

## 1. FTC affiliate disclosure (16 CFR §255, Endorsement Guides)
- Any page with an affiliate link needs a disclosure that is **clear, conspicuous,
  and requires no click/hover to see** — the "Four Ps": Prominence, Placement,
  Permanence, Proximity.
- Disclosure text must be near the top of content (not buried in a footer), in a font
  size/contrast at least as visible as body text, and repeated near each affiliate
  link block, not just once per page.
- Suggested standard component text (adapt, don't ship verbatim as legal copy without
  review): "Advertising Disclosure: We may earn a commission if you purchase a policy
  through links on this page. This doesn't affect our editorial opinions. See our
  [Advertising Disclosure] page for details."
- A separate, linked full Advertising/Affiliate Disclosure page is standard practice
  alongside the inline disclosures.

## 2. Insurance-producer licensing risk
Research summary (verify current status before launch, rules vary and enforcement has
tightened):
- Pure SEO/content lead-generation — i.e., publishing informational content and
  routing users to carriers/comparison partners without quoting, soliciting, or
  negotiating insurance terms yourself — has generally **not** required a producer
  license under most state adaptations of the NAIC Model Producer Licensing Act,
  as long as the site doesn't cross into "selling, soliciting, or negotiating"
  insurance itself.
- The line matters: don't display carrier-specific premium quotes generated by your
  own logic, don't let users "buy" on-site, don't imply you are the insurer or an
  agent. Route to the carrier/partner for the actual quote/purchase.
- Some platforms (notably Google Ads) impose their **own** stricter requirement —
  e.g., requiring an insurance producer license to advertise health-insurance-related
  content — separate from state law. If paid ads are ever added, check current
  platform ad policies for the insurance category before running any paid traffic;
  this project is currently organic/SEO-only, which avoids that specific trigger.
- Add a clear disclaimer: "[Site name] is not an insurance agency and does not sell
  insurance policies. We provide informational content and may be compensated for
  referrals to third-party insurance providers." This should live in the footer
  site-wide and on an About/Disclosure page.

## 3. State-specific "not licensed/available in all states" language
- Where a state page or carrier page discusses availability, include a disclaimer
  along the lines of: "Not all insurance products are available in every state.
  Availability and terms vary by state and provider — confirm details directly with
  the insurance carrier." (Several state DOI opinions specifically expect this kind
  of disclaimer on advertisements for out-of-state/non-authorized products.)

## 4. Privacy / data collection
- If the site collects any personal data (email capture, quote-request forms, the
  cost-estimator tool if it stores inputs): needs a Privacy Policy, and depending on
  traffic/state mix, CCPA/CPRA (California) considerations — "Do Not Sell/Share My
  Personal Information" link if data is shared with partners in a way that qualifies
  as a "sale/share" under CCPA.
- If any SMS/call-based lead capture is ever added (not in current scope), TCPA
  consent requirements apply — flag explicitly if scope changes, this is a
  high-enforcement-risk area.
- Cookie/consent banner if using analytics/ad tech that sets tracking cookies,
  especially for any EU/UK visitors incidentally reached.

## 5. Content accuracy / UDAP risk
- Avoid unsubstantiated superlatives ("#1 rated", "guaranteed lowest price") without
  a defensible basis — this is both an FTC truth-in-advertising issue and a
  state-insurance-advertising issue (unfair/deceptive acts and practices, UDAP,
  statutes exist in every state and apply to insurance advertising specifically).
- Keep carrier comparisons factual and sourced; update review/comparison content
  periodically since coverage terms and pricing change.

## 6. Domain reuse — trademark note (see also project brief §4)
- Do a basic USPTO TESS search for the working name before heavy investment; the
  "___ Genius" naming pattern is associated with an existing insurance-comparison
  brand (Policygenius), which raises trademark-adjacent risk even if the current
  domain string is arguably descriptive/generic. This is a business decision for the
  owner, not something Codex can resolve — flag it, don't block on it.

## 7. Pre-launch checklist (quick reference)
- [ ] Advertising/affiliate disclosure page published + linked in footer + inline
      disclosures on every monetized page
- [ ] "Not an insurance agency" disclaimer in footer site-wide
- [ ] "Not available in all states" style language on state/carrier pages
- [ ] Privacy Policy published; CCPA opt-out link if applicable
- [ ] No fabricated reviews, ratings, or urgency mechanics
- [ ] No on-site "buy" flow or carrier-specific quote generation — route out to
      partners for actual quotes/purchase
- [ ] Trademark spot-check on the working brand name completed by the owner
- [ ] Backlink/history audit of the domain completed by the owner
