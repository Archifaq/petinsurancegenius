For senior dogs, the Pets Best vs MetLife comparison should begin with enrollment rules and waiting periods, not a winner label. Older dogs often have longer medical records, so the same quote can look very different after the provider reviews symptoms, diagnoses, and prior treatment.

## Pets Best: Senior Eligibility Questions

Pets Best says it has no upper age limit for enrollment, while also making clear that pre-existing conditions are not covered. That combination matters for senior dogs: age alone may not close the door, but the dog's medical history can still shape what future claims are eligible.

The useful Pets Best questions are practical: which accident-and-illness plan is being quoted, whether optional wellness is being added, what annual limit is selected, and whether Direct Vet Pay or ordinary reimbursement fits the owner's cash-flow needs.

## MetLife: Waiting Period and Record Questions

MetLife Pet states that accident coverage can begin with a 0-day waiting period, while illness coverage begins after a 14-day waiting period. MetLife also describes pre-existing conditions broadly around injuries or illnesses that were contracted, manifested, diagnosed, or treated before coverage or during applicable waiting periods.

For a senior dog, that wording makes the records review central. Ask how far back records may be requested, what counts as a symptom, and whether a past issue could affect a future claim even if the owner never received a formal diagnosis.

## A Senior-Dog Comparison Order

First compare eligibility and pre-existing-condition wording. Second, compare waiting periods for accidents, illnesses, and any condition category that matters to your dog. Third, compare annual limits, reimbursement levels, deductibles, and claim-payment workflow.

Only after that should monthly premium become the deciding factor. A senior dog page that starts with price can miss the policy language most likely to matter after a claim is filed.
